The Long Balance

Spend the light. Read the ground. Keep the fire.

iPhone  ·  iPad  ·  Apple Vision Pro

You arrive in a valley with nothing, and no one is coming. Each morning gives you a fixed number of hours, and the only question the game ever asks is what those hours buy.

Fire warms. Rain soaks. Hunger builds, and a wound left alone turns bad. Nothing here is abstracted into a health bar or a resource counter — the cold is a real thermal budget, the larder is real food that really rots, and the ground remembers what you took out of it.

There is no score. Live long enough, and the place earns a name.

Five ways of looking at one valley

The instrument you build

The sky tells you when

Raise the plate against the stars and it is an astrolabe cut for this valley: the rings drawn for your own latitude, the sun's whole arc from rising to setting, and the two limits the year swings between.

It shows only what your camp has earned. A bare limb at first. The tropics once the notched stone stands. The fine divisions only when there is ink to keep the count.

Three rules the whole game obeys

  1. Everything you see is true.What a person wears says what you have made. Black earth on the plan is black earth in the world. Nothing is decoration.
  2. The same day plays the same way.The simulation is deterministic. Your valley is not a roll of the dice — it is a place, and it will still be that place tomorrow.
  3. Take, and give back.Cut every tree and the rain that the forest sponged now strips your soil. Build so the valley is richer for your being in it.
